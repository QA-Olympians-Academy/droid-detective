# Live 1 — Name your own three

**Slot:** 00:00 · Ch1 Opening (talk) · **Time:** 3 min · **Needs:** nothing — paper only
**Format:** 90 s solo → 90 s in pairs

---

## Do this now

Think of the **last test that broke on you** — one you actually had to fix.

Write three lines:

1. What broke it? (a rename · a new dialog · a timing change · something else)
2. Was there a **real bug** behind it? Yes / No
3. Which of these three does it belong to?
   - **A** — static locator in a moving UI
   - **B** — scripted steps for a reasoning problem
   - **C** — knowledge that lived in one person's head

Turn to your neighbour and swap answers. 45 seconds each.

---

## What you should get

Almost every hand goes to **A**, and almost every answer to question 2 is **No**.

That is the argument for the next three hours: the tests weren't wrong and the product
wasn't broken. The automation was structurally brittle — and brittleness is a property of
the approach, not a skill gap.

If someone lands on **C**, keep their example. It is the best one to come back to in
Chapter 7, when that knowledge becomes a reasoning trace instead of a person.

---

## Instructor cue

- Show of hands per category **before** anyone speaks. The A-column is the slide.
- One volunteer reads theirs out. Don't fix it — just name the category.
- **Behind schedule?** Drop the pair swap, keep the show of hands. 60 seconds.
