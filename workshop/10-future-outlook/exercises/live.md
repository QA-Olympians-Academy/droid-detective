# Live 10 — Pick one, name the barrier

**Slot:** 02:55 · Ch10 Wrap-up (talk) · **Time:** 3 min · **Needs:** nothing — paper, then room
**Format:** 60 s solo → show of hands → two answers out loud

---

> **The last block is 5 minutes for Ch9 + Ch10 + Ch11.** Run **one** of Live 9, 10 or 11 —
> not all three. This one lands best with a senior or mixed-role room.

## Do this now

Four directions this is heading:

1. 👥 **Multi-agent teams** — a coordinator routing narrow specialists
2. 🔁 **Learning from outcomes** — N green runs promote a healed selector to trusted
3. 💬 **Authoring at scale** — a user story in, a reviewable scenario tree out
4. 🛡️ **Guardrails by default** — allowlists, step caps, review gates

Write two lines:

- Which one would pay off **first** for your team, and why?
- What is the **trust barrier** that stops you adopting it on Monday?

---

## What you should get

The barrier is almost never model capability — models can do most of this today. It is
**trust infrastructure**: can you tell where a generated test came from, diff it against a
human-written one, and see its confidence before you approve it?

If the room is honest, the answers cluster on review capacity and on who is accountable when
an agent's patch is wrong. Both are policy questions, not engineering ones — which is the
note to end on: the guardrails you need are **decisions you have to make explicitly**, not
features you are waiting for.

## Instructor cue

- Show of hands on the four directions first — it takes 20 seconds and tells you which two answers to ask for out loud.
- Do not resolve the trust question. Leave it open; it is the thing they take back to their team.
- **Behind schedule?** Show of hands only, no discussion. 45 seconds.
